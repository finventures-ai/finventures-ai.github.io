## Short answer

- **Choose Synthesia** if you produce training, onboarding or explainer videos of several minutes: its free Basic plan gives 10 watermarked minutes a month, and Starter is $19/month or $14/month billed annually.
- **Choose HeyGen** if you produce short vertical clips for social media: its free plan gives 3 watermarked videos a month of up to 1 minute each, and Creator is $29/month or $24/month billed annually.
- **Test both free plans with the same script** before paying; the right choice is the one whose output suits your audience, which no table can tell you.

## Same technology, different jobs

Both tools put a digital presenter on screen reading your script, in many languages. The differences that matter are in the limits and the positioning.

**Synthesia** focuses on business video: training, onboarding and internal communication. Its free plan is measured in minutes (10 a month), which suits a few longer videos. Its Pro plan adds personal avatars and translations into many languages, per its pricing page.

**HeyGen** focuses on creators and marketers. Its free plan is measured in videos (3 a month, up to a minute each), which suits testing short formats. Its paid plans add your own custom avatar with your voice, longer videos and higher-resolution export.

## Understanding credits

Both vendors now bill paid plans in credits, so the headline price does not tell you how many videos you get.

- **Synthesia:** per-minute credit use (avatar video, dubbing) and allowance rollover rules change; confirm on [synthesia.io/pricing](https://www.synthesia.io/pricing) (and help linked from that page).
- **HeyGen:** monthly credit allotments by tier (including Creator) and month-to-month rollover are listed on [heygen.com/pricing](https://www.heygen.com/pricing); credits per video depend on model and length.

Before paying, make one representative video on the free plan and note how much of the allowance it used. That is a better estimate than any table.

## Consent and responsible use

Only create an avatar or clone a voice that is yours or used with explicit permission. Both vendors describe content moderation on their pages. We have not reviewed their licence terms for commercial use; read them before monetising videos.

## Who should skip both

If you are comfortable on camera, filming yourself and using an editor is often cheaper and more personal. If your content is mostly narration over visuals, a dedicated voice tool may be the better first purchase; we have not compared one here.

## Final verdict

The evidence: Synthesia offers more free minutes and a cheaper entry tier; HeyGen offers a free plan shaped for short clips and a creator-oriented paid tier.
