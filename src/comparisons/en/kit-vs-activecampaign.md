## Short answer

- **Choose Kit** if your business is the newsletter or the audience itself (writing, video, courses sold to an audience) and you want to start at $0: its free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with no stated sending limit.
- **Choose ActiveCampaign** if email is one part of a sales process: lead follow-up, pipelines, scoring, and automations that react to what contacts do. It has no permanent free plan; you evaluate it with a 14-day trial.
- **The deciding question:** do you need a CRM? If you do not know what you would put in one, you probably do not need it yet.

## Two different problems

These tools are often compared because both send email, but they answer different questions.

**Kit answers "how do I grow and keep an audience?"** Tags, sequences, a free plan generous enough to grow on, and features aimed at creators. If your revenue comes from people who read or watch you (sponsorships, digital products, paid newsletters), that is the core job.

**ActiveCampaign answers "how do I follow up with every lead and customer automatically?"** Automations that branch on behaviour, a CRM with deals and stages, and reporting around them. If you sell a service where a lead becomes a call becomes a proposal, that is the core job.

Picking the wrong one is expensive in time, not only money: a creator who starts in a CRM-oriented tool spends weeks learning features they never use, and a service business that starts in a newsletter tool ends up stitching a separate CRM onto it.

## Free use in more depth

### Kit

Kit's FAQ describes the free plan as the essential tools to build an audience of up to 10,000 subscribers, and states there is no sending limit on any plan. Which automations and integrations are restricted on the free plan is not confirmed in our sources; build the workflow you need in a free account before relying on it. Paid plans (Creator, Pro) add features; Kit offers a 14-day trial without a card and refunds within the first 30 days.

### ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign has no permanent free plan. Its 14-day trial gives access to Pro-type features, and its help center describes a limited number of sends during the trial. The exact cap is not confirmed in our table: check the help center before you rely on it. That is enough to build and test an automation with your own addresses, not to run a real campaign.

## Paid plans

Both vendors price by list size. Kit's Creator price changes with your subscriber count. ActiveCampaign's pricing page shows a "starts at" figure that also changes with contacts; an ActiveCampaign blog post once cited a lower entry figure, but we have not confirmed a current amount, so we do not publish one. Compare for your own numbers on [Kit pricing](https://kit.com/pricing) and [ActiveCampaign pricing](https://www.activecampaign.com/pricing).

If you want pages, checkout and email in one free tool, see Systeme.io in our [funnel builder guide](/en/guides/choose-funnel-builder-small-business/). If budget is the main constraint and you send occasionally, see [Kit vs Brevo](/en/vs/kit-vs-brevo/).

## Final verdict

The evidence: Kit has a free plan up to 10,000 subscribers; ActiveCampaign has a 14-day trial and an automation-and-CRM core. Our opinion: **audience businesses should start on Kit; sales-process businesses should trial ActiveCampaign** and only pay once the trial automation mirrors their real follow-up.
