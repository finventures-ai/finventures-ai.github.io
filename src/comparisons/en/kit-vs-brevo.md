## Short answer

- **Choose Kit** if you are building a newsletter or creator audience from scratch and expect your subscriber count to grow faster than your sending: its free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers, and its FAQ states there is no sending limit on any plan.
- **Choose Brevo** if what limits you is how many emails you send per day rather than how many people are on your list, for example occasional updates to an existing customer base: its free plan allows up to 300 emails a day, with no card.
- **It depends** on one number you can estimate today: how many emails you will send in your busiest day.

## Two opposite ways to limit a free plan

This is the heart of the comparison. Most email tools pick one of two levers to push you to a paid plan.

**Kit limits the list size.** Up to 10,000 subscribers you can stay free, and the FAQ states there is no sending limit on any plan. If you publish a weekly newsletter to 3,000 people, Kit's free plan covers that.

**Brevo limits the daily send.** Its free plan allows up to 300 emails a day once your account is approved. If your list is 3,000 people and you want to email all of them on the same day, 300 a day is a real constraint: a single newsletter would take ten days to go out. If instead you send a few hundred transactional-style or follow-up emails spread over the week, the daily cap may never bother you.

The practical test: take your largest planned send (usually your newsletter) and divide it by 300. If the result is more than one day, Brevo's free plan will not carry your newsletter.

## Free plans in more depth

### Kit

Kit's pricing FAQ describes the free plan as the essential tools to build and engage an audience of up to 10,000 subscribers. What our sources do not confirm is the exact set of automations, integrations and page features restricted on the free plan. If a specific automation matters to you, build it in a free account before committing.

### Brevo

Brevo's free plan requires no card and allows up to 300 emails a day after account approval. Brevo presents itself as a broader marketing platform rather than a newsletter tool; our tool table records automations as available and CRM as partial, but we have not recorded the per-plan feature split. Check [Brevo pricing](https://www.brevo.com/pricing) for what each tier includes.

## Paid plans

Neither vendor shows a single fixed entry price for every situation. Kit's Creator price changes with your subscriber count; Brevo's Starter is described by monthly email volume (its marketing mentions tiers such as 5,000 emails a month). We did not record exact amounts, so compare on [Kit pricing](https://kit.com/pricing) and [Brevo pricing](https://www.brevo.com/pricing) with your own list size and sending volume.

Kit offers a 14-day trial of Creator and Pro without a card, and refunds within the first 30 days.

## Product philosophy

**Kit** is built for creators: tags rather than separate lists, visual automations, and creator-focused features on paid plans. Its marketing targets writers, YouTubers and newsletter publishers.

**Brevo** is built as a general marketing platform for businesses, with email as one component. That breadth can be useful if you also want CRM-style contact management; it can also mean more interface to learn if all you want is a newsletter. This is our reading of each vendor's positioning, not a test result.

## Final verdict

The evidence: Kit caps subscribers at 10,000 with no stated sending limit; Brevo caps sending at 300 a day. Our opinion: **for a newsletter, Kit's model is the safer free start; for occasional sends to an existing contact base, Brevo's model can fit.** Run your busiest-day number through both before choosing.
