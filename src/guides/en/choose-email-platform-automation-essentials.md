## Who this guide is for

You run a small business, a coaching practice or a creator project, and you know you need an email list. Every comparison you open leads with price and subscriber caps. Those matter, but they are the second question. The first is: **what does your list need to do automatically?** A tool that is free but cannot run the one sequence your business depends on is not free; it is a detour.

This guide walks through the automations most small lists actually need, then shows how the free plans and trials of five common tools line up against them. If you only want the free-plan numbers, our [free email tools roundup](/en/guides/best-free-email-marketing-tools-2026/) covers that faster.

## Step 1: Write down the five automations you will really use

Most small lists run on a short set of automations. Check which ones apply to you before you open a pricing page.

1. **Welcome sequence.** Someone signs up; they receive three to five emails over a week or two. Almost every list needs this.
2. **Lead-magnet delivery.** The signup form promises a guide or checklist; the first email delivers it. Often merged with the welcome sequence.
3. **Tag or segment by interest.** A subscriber who downloads guide A should not get the same follow-up as one who downloaded guide B. This is where free plans start to pinch.
4. **Behaviour triggers.** Clicked a link, bought a product, abandoned a checkout. Useful once you sell something; rarely needed on day one.
5. **CRM-style follow-up.** Sales pipelines, deal stages, lead scoring. Valuable for service businesses with a sales process; overkill for a newsletter.

If your list only needs 1 and 2, nearly any tool on this page will do. If you need 3, check the automation and tag limits carefully. If you need 4 and 5, you are likely looking at a paid plan, whatever the marketing says.

## Step 2: Estimate where your list will be in 12 months

Free plans fail in two ways: you hit a **subscriber cap**, or you hit a **sending cap**. Make a rough, honest guess at your list size a year from now. Not a goal, a guess. Then look for the plan whose limit you will reach last.

## Reading the table with your needs in mind

**If you need a welcome sequence and lead-magnet delivery only.** Kit's free plan gives the most subscriber headroom. MailerLite's free plan documents more included tools (a site, a landing page, forms) but caps you at 250 subscribers; on a free account, sending is blocked once active subscribers exceed that number. Systeme.io's single free automation rule and single campaign are enough for exactly one signup-to-sequence flow, which is precisely this use case.

**If you need several sequences by interest.** This is the most common reason people outgrow a free plan. MailerLite's three active automations allow a little branching. Systeme.io's free plan allows one rule, so a second lead magnet with its own sequence generally means its $17 Startup plan. Kit's free automation limits are not confirmed in our sources; test before you commit.

**If you send in small batches or irregularly.** Brevo's free cap is per day rather than per subscriber, which suits some patterns, such as occasional updates to an existing customer list. Note that the 300-per-day limit applies after account approval.

**If automation and CRM are the point.** ActiveCampaign is built around automation and CRM and has no permanent free plan. Use the 14-day trial to build the exact workflow you need, and only then look at the price for your contact count.

**If you also need pages, a checkout or a course.** Systeme.io combines email with funnels, a course and a blog on one free plan. It is not the deepest email automation tool here, but it removes the need to connect a separate page builder.

## Step 4: Run a two-week test, not a feature debate

1. Open free accounts (or a trial) on your top two candidates.
2. Build the same thing in both: one form, one tag, a three-email welcome sequence.
3. Time yourself. The tool you finish faster in is usually the one you will keep using.
4. Send the sequence to two or three of your own addresses at different providers and note where it lands. This is not a deliverability study, but it catches obvious setup problems.
5. Pick one tool for real signups. Splitting a small audience across two tools gives you meaningless numbers.

## Two setup facts that matter more than the tool

**Your sending domain.** Mailbox providers increasingly expect senders to use their own authenticated domain. Systeme.io, for example, states in its help center that a Gmail address can no longer be used as a sender. Whichever tool you pick, plan for an address on your own domain before your first real send.

**Consent and identification.** In Canada, CASL requires consent, sender identification with a postal address, and a working unsubscribe link in every commercial email; the US has similar CAN-SPAM rules. Every tool here provides an unsubscribe mechanism; consent records and your footer details remain your responsibility.

## Who this approach is not for

If you already run a large list with complex e-commerce flows, a decision guide for small lists will be too basic. Compare the paid tiers directly on vendor pages, ideally with a migration quote.
