## Who this guide is for

You want to publish short videos for your business without filming yourself every time, and AI avatar tools look like the answer. The tools are the easy part. The hard part is that a confident avatar reading a wrong price, a made-up statistic or an unlabelled promotion does more damage than no video at all.

This guide is a workflow, not a tool ranking. If you want to compare tools first, read our [AI video tools roundup](/en/guides/best-ai-video-tools/). Here we focus on the order of operations that keeps your videos accurate, disclosed and consistent.

## The workflow at a glance

1. Collect and label your facts.
2. Write the script from the labelled facts.
3. Choose the tool by format, not by hype.
4. Produce a test version on a free plan.
5. Add captions, disclosure and a single call to action.
6. Review against a short checklist.
7. Publish, measure, and only then pay for the format that works.

## Step 1: Collect and label your facts before writing a word

Every number that will appear on screen or in the voiceover should come from a source you can name: an official pricing page, a help-center article, your own sales data. Note the date you read it.

Give each fact a label:

- **VERIFIED**: read on the official page, with a date.
- **UNVERIFIED**: from a secondary source or memory; do not say it on camera.
- **OPINION**: your recommendation; say it as an opinion ("we would start with…").

This step takes ten minutes and prevents the most common AI-video mistake: an AI writing assistant inventing a plausible figure that ends up spoken by an avatar.

## Step 2: Script from the labelled facts

A short video script that works for most business topics:

- **Hook (first 3 seconds):** the problem or the surprising fact (VERIFIED only).
- **Three points:** each one sentence, each traceable to your fact list.
- **One call to action:** one link or one comment keyword, not three.
- **Disclosure line** if a link earns you anything.

If you use an AI writing assistant to draft, paste your fact list into the prompt and ask it to use only those facts and to flag anything it adds. Then check the draft line by line against the list.

## Step 4: Produce a test version on a free plan

Use the free tier to answer one question: does this format suit your audience? Watermarks do not matter at this stage. Make two or three versions of the same script with different hooks rather than three different topics, so you learn what actually changed.

## Step 5: Captions, disclosure, one call to action

- **Captions:** many short-form viewers watch without sound; put the key sentence on screen.
- **Disclosure:** if a link can earn you a commission, say so in the video and the caption, for example "Affiliate link: we may earn a commission at no cost to you." This follows the spirit of FTC guidance in the US and Competition Bureau guidance in Canada. Also use each platform's branded-content setting where it applies.
- **AI disclosure:** some platforms ask creators to label realistic AI-generated content. Check the current rules of each platform you publish on; we have not reviewed them for this guide.
- **One call to action:** end with a single, clear next step.

## Step 6: Review before publishing

A five-point checklist we suggest running on every clip:

1. Every number on screen or in the voiceover is on the VERIFIED list, with its date.
2. Nothing implies a personal test, a result or a testimonial that did not happen.
3. The disclosure is present wherever a link may earn a commission.
4. The avatar and voice are your own, stock assets, or used with explicit permission.
5. The call to action points to a page that exists and loads.

## Step 7: Publish, measure, then pay

Run the free version for two weeks. Look at watch time and replies, not only views. Upgrade only the tool behind the format that performs, and only the plan you need.

## Consent and likeness

Only clone a voice or a face that you own or have explicit permission to use. Both HeyGen and Synthesia describe content moderation on their pages; read each vendor's acceptable-use policy before creating an avatar of a real person.

## Who this workflow is not for

If you produce long-form documentaries, live-action ads or anything where a human presenter's credibility is the product, avatar tools may be the wrong medium. Film yourself and use an editor instead.

## Our recommendation, separated from the evidence

The evidence: the free-plan limits and listed prices above. Our opinion: **the workflow matters more than the tool.** Start with the fact list and the review checklist; pick HeyGen for short social clips or Synthesia for structured training; stay on the free plan until one format has proven itself.
