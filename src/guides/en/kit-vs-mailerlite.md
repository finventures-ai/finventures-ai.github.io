### MailerLite free

MailerLite is unusually explicit about its free plan. Per mailerlite.com/pricing:

- 250 subscribers, 2,500 monthly emails;
- campaigns, free automations (3 active, up to 5 steps), 1 website and 1 landing page, 3 active forms;
- all three editors: drag & drop, custom HTML and simple;

- 100+ integrations; API and MCP access are limited on free and don't include sending.

The critical rule: **"For free accounts, when the number of active subscribers exceeds the 250 active subscriber limit, sendings will be locked (campaigns + automation)."** Plan your upgrade before you reach 250.

## A 30-day test you can run yourself

If you're still undecided, both tools can be tested at $0, so let your own data decide:

1. **Days 1–2:** open a free account on each. Import nothing yet.
2. **Days 3–5:** build the same signup form and the same three-email welcome sequence in both. Time yourself. The tool you finish faster in is the one you'll actually keep using.
3. **Days 6–7:** send the test sequence to two or three of your own addresses on different providers (Gmail, Outlook, a work address). Note where each email lands. This is not a deliverability study, but it catches obvious setup problems such as a missing domain authentication.
4. **Days 8–30:** run real signups through one tool only (splitting a tiny audience produces meaningless numbers). Track signup rate, first-email open rate and clicks.
5. **Day 30:** decide. If you're likely to cross 250 subscribers within a few months and don't want to pay, Kit's free ceiling probably settles it. If MailerLite's editor and site saved you hours, its Comfort plan may be worth the price.

Whatever you choose, keep a CSV export of your list every month. It's free insurance against any account problem.
